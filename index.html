Overview

01 · Core Idea

Core Idea

Glycosides are organic compounds that yield, on hydrolysis, one or more sugar molecules (glycon) and a non-sugar moiety (aglycon / genin). The glycon controls solubility, absorption and distribution (kinetics), while the aglycon is responsible for the pharmacological activity. Glycosides are classified by the linkage (O-, S-, N-, C-) and by the chemical structure of the aglycon. This lecture covers the general introduction and then the cardiac (cardioactive) glycosides: steroidal aglycons with an unsaturated lactone ring at C-17 that act by inhibiting Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, with Digitalis, Nerium oleander and squill as the plant sources.

02 · Learning Points

What you should leave with

  • Define a glycoside and name its two parts: glycon and aglycon
  • Know that glycosides are chemically acetals (“sugar ethers”) and that only the β form occurs naturally in plants
  • Explain glycoside stability (acids, alkalis, enzymes) and the functions of glycosides in plants
  • Classify glycosides by linkage (O, S, N, C) and by the aglycon type
  • Describe the structure–activity features of cardioactive aglycons and the two lactone types (cardenolides vs. bufadienolides)
  • Explain the mechanism of action of cardiac glycosides and their use in CHF
  • Apply the nomenclature steps to a cardioactive glycoside
  • Know the plants: Digitalis purpurea, Digitalis lanata, Nerium oleander, squill (white / red), their constituents and uses
  • Recognize oleandrin poisoning and its supportive treatment

05 · Important Concepts

Alpha and beta glycosides

  • Both α and β orientations are possible because of the chiral carbon 1 of the sugar (the acetal carbon)
  • It depends on the stereo-configuration of the glycosidic linkage
  • Only β forms occur naturally in plants

Glycoside stability

  • Usually hydrolyzed by acids and stable to alkalis; some are more resistant to hydrolysis than others
  • Most are easily hydrolyzed by glycosidases into glycon and aglycon
  • Plant enzymes sit in cells that do not contain glycosides, but can be released to hydrolyze the glycoside after injury or improper extraction
  • Examples: emulsin (bitter almond seeds), myrosine (black mustard seeds)

Roles of glycon and aglycon

  • Glycon: gives solubility properties → important in absorption and distribution (kinetics)
  • Aglycon: responsible for the pharmacological activity
  • Most glycosides dissolve in water or hydro-alcoholic solutions because of the sugar part

Functions of glycosides in plant tissue

  • Detoxification (converting toxic material into less toxic ones)
  • Defense mechanism (antibacterial and antiseptic, due to the aglycon)
  • Sugar reserve (a way to store sugar as a source of energy)
  • Pigmentation
  • Storage of toxins (phenolic compounds)

Cardioactive aglycon — pharmacologic action

  • Highly specific action on cardiac muscle: increases tone, excitability and contractility
  • Aglycons are steroidal: derivatives of cyclopentaphenanthrene with an unsaturated lactone ring at C-17
  • The sugar is always linked at C-3 (sugar–O at C-3 in the lecture figure)

Mechanism of action

  • Act at the membrane level by inhibiting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase
  • Result: ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ in the myocardium and conduction system (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger shown in the figure) → ↑ TN-C Ca²⁺ binding → ↑ inotropy
  • Inotropy is increased while conduction velocity is reduced
  • In CHF: ↑ cardiac output, ↑ cardiac work capacity without increased oxygen consumption, and ↓ heart rate
  • Digoxin is nowadays the most widely used drug in CHF

Structural features of all cardioactive glycosides

  1. β-OH at C-3, always involved in the glycosidic linkage to a mono-, di-, tri- or tetrasaccharide
  2. Another β-OH at C-14
  3. α,β-unsaturated 5- or 6-membered lactone ring at C-17, also in the β configuration
  4. Cyclopentaphenanthrene ring junctions: A/B usually cis, B/C always trans, C/D always cis
  5. Additional OH groups may be present at C-5, C-11 and C-16
REQUIREMENTS FOR ACTIVITY
  • A steroidal compound becomes cardio-active only when it has the α,β-unsaturated lactone ring at C-17β; without this ring it is inactive as a cardio-drug
  • For maximal activity: β-OH at C-14 makes the glycoside very active with rapid action (lack of this OH greatly reduces activity)
  • Sugar at β-C-3-OH increases absorption and distribution (e.g. glucose, rhamnose, digitoxose)
  • Ring junctions cis–trans–cis make the nucleus very stable, thus more active

07 · Processes / Stages

Biosynthesis of cardioactive aglycons

  1. Acetate–mevalonate biosynthesis pathway
  2. Squalene → Cholesterol
  3. Cholesterol → Pregnenolone
  4. Pregnenolone + C2 unit → cardenolide, or + C3 unit → bufadienolide

Nomenclature of cardioactive glycosides

  1. Number the structure
  2. Arrange the functional groups starting from the least number
  3. Denote whether α or β
  4. Denote the type (cardenolide or bufadienolide, from the lactone ring)
  5. Denote the position and number of double bonds and groups

Worked nomenclature example (lecture)

3β, 14β, 16β – trihydroxy – 4α – methoxy – Card-1,20(22) – di – en – olide

  • “Card” = cardenolide (5-membered lactone)
  • 1,20(22) = position of the double bonds; di = number of double bonds

Squill — collection

  • The bulb grows half immersed in sandy soil near the sea and is gathered in August
  • Membranous outer scales and the central portion are removed; the fleshy scales are cut into transient pieces and dried
  • India is now the major producer of squill

Structures shown in the lecture

  • Glycosides: sennoside, emodin-O-rhamnoside, digoxin (digitoxose sugars + cardenolide steroid)
  • Linkage examples: aloin (C), digoxin (O), adenosine (N), sinigrin (S)
  • Genins: digitoxigenin, gitoxigenin, digoxigenin, diginatigenin; gitaloxin, oleandrin, scillaren A
09 · Clinical / Practical Importance

Clinical uses

  • Digoxin — the most widely used drug in CHF; from D. lanata, more rapidly absorbed than purpurea glycosides, so valuable for rapid treatment of CHF
  • Squill — expectorant with cardiotonic and diuretic properties (usual dose 100 mg)
  • Oleander — has been used to treat cardiac insufficiency
  • Oleandrin as anti-cancer therapy — a promising agent; in vitro potential in cancers of the colon, lung, leukemia, pancreas, melanoma and prostate; may act as a cytotoxic agent, generating reactive oxygen species or inducing apoptosis, and has shown synergy with current chemotherapy

Oleandrin poisoning — symptoms

  • Gastrointestinal: nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, higher salivation, diarrhea (may contain blood)
  • Cardiac (after the first symptoms): tachyarrhythmia, bradyarrhythmia
  • CNS: drowsiness, tremors, seizures

Supportive treatment

  • Consider a single dose of activated charcoal to bind any oleandrin left in the GI tract (reduces absorption)
  • Supportive care: monitor vitals, electrolytes and fluid balance; patients may be hypovolemic due to diarrhea and vomiting
10 · Exam Focus

Must remember

  • Glycoside = glycon (sugar) + aglycon / genin; chemically acetals / sugar ethers; most frequent sugar β-glucose
  • Only β forms occur naturally in plants (chiral C-1, acetal carbon)
  • Hydrolyzed by acids and glycosidases; stable to alkalis; emulsin (bitter almond), myrosine (black mustard)
  • Glycon = solubility / kinetics; aglycon = pharmacological activity
  • Plant functions: detoxification, defense, sugar reserve, pigmentation, storage of toxins
  • Linkage: O (digoxin, most common), S (sinigrin), N (adenosine), C (aloin)
  • Cardiac aglycon: steroidal cyclopentaphenanthrene + unsaturated lactone at C-17; sugar at C-3; 14β-OH
  • Ring junctions A/B usually cis, B/C trans, C/D cis
  • Cardenolide = 5-membered, C23 (digitoxigenin); bufadienolide = 6-membered, C24 (scillarenin)
  • Mechanism: inhibition of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ → ↑ inotropy; conduction velocity reduced
  • Biosynthesis: acetate–mevalonate → cholesterol → pregnenolone → +C2 cardenolide / +C3 bufadienolide
  • D. lanata contains digoxigenin and diginatigenin (not in D. purpurea) and is the source of digoxin
  • Oleandrin = acetyl derivative of gitoxigenin; poisoning → GI, cardiac and CNS effects; treat with activated charcoal + supportive care
  • Scillaren A (white squill) → scillarenin + rhamnose + glucose; red squill = rat poison
